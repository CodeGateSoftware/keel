### 69.1 "7 Habits of a Highly Successful Trader" (Mark Crisp, stressfreetrading.com, self-published ebook, 31pp)

Seven short chapters (Take Complete Responsibility · Have a System That Fits You · Plan a Trade and
Trade a Plan · Work Hard at Learning How to Trade Properly · Positive Self-Belief · View Trading as a
Score in Points and Not in Money · Keep Trading as Part of a Balanced Life) plus a conclusion. Pure
discipline/psychology content, no indicators, no rules, no backtests — "follow your rules," "don't ask
for outside opinions," "view P&L as points not dollars," references to Market Wizards and Nicolas
Darvas as inspiration. Every page read (full document).

**Verdict: discarded — no agent value.** Entirely subsumed by the psychology saturation already logged
(§23/§24/§26/§57): rule-following discipline, no-averaging-down, plan-before-entry are all already
either hard rails (`guards.py`) or promotion-floor logic. Nothing mechanically new.

### 69.2 "Secrets of Millionaire Traders" (NetFutures Inc./Zaner Group, in association with John Piper's "The Way to Trade", ~2000s bonus report, 27pp)

A 25-numbered-rule "secrets" bonus report distilled from interviews with unnamed "millionaire
traders" — use risk capital only, know yourself, start small, don't over-commit (3x margin buffer),
isolate trading from hope, don't form opinions mid-session, don't follow the crowd, avoid market
orders, trade the most active futures contract month, trade divergence between related commodity
"families" (grains/meats/metals), don't trade too many markets at once, trade opening-range/
previous-day/week/month-range breakouts, build a pyramid (decreasing size), never enter a full
position at one price, never add to a losing position, cut losses short, let profits run, be
impatient with losers, learn to like losses. Every page read (full document).

**Verdict: discarded — no agent value.** Every substantive rule is either (a) generic risk-management
wisdom already saturated many times over in this KB (cut losses/let profits run/never average
down — already rails 5, 8, 9, 10 and §57.4's negative exemplar), or (b) structurally futures/
day-trading-specific and non-transferable: margin-account sizing (riba, excluded), trading the "most
active contract month" (futures expiry cycles — N/A to spot crypto, which has no contract months),
divergence across commodity "families" (needs 3+ correlated-but-distinct assets in visible groups; our
allowlist is BTC/ETH/PAXG, too narrow for this to mean anything), and the various intraday/daily/
weekly *range-breakout* rules are a strictly weaker, unparametrized restatement of the already-shipped
Donchian/Turtle breakout (§23.1/§27.1/§54.14) — nothing here sharpens it. No new mechanically testable
idea survives translation.

### 69.3 "18 Trading Champions Share Their Keys to Top Trading Profits" (FWN — News for the Financial World, 1996 trader-profile compilation, 20pp)

A magazine-style compilation of eighteen short (~1 page each) interview profiles of individual
futures/options traders: George Angell, Jake Bernstein, Tom Bierovic, Walter Bressert, Tom DeMark,
George Fontanills, Lee Gettess, Cynthia Kase, George Lane, Glenn Neely, Grant Noble, Linda Bradford
Raschke, Rick Redmont, Angelo Reynolds, Joe Stowell, Gary Wagner, Ben Warwick, Larry Williams. Each is
biographical/anecdotal — "what markets do you trade," "what's your edge," "advice for beginners" — not
a systematic rule set, and none report backtest statistics. Every page read (full document).

**Verdict: discarded — no agent value.** This is discretionary trader biography, not extractable
mechanism. Techniques name-checked are either already in the KB (Kase's Dev-Stop volatility-adjusted
stop is already §54.6/§54.8; candlestick sentiment reading = §55/Source 59; Elliott wave = deferred/
no-oracle; stochastics = declined per KISS §57.5) or structurally excluded (Reynolds scalps the
Eurodollar futures pit — an interest-rate instrument, riba, plus scalping; Fontanills trades
delta-neutral options spreads; Redmont trades OEX options "98% of the time"). Nothing survives as a
new, testable, halal-compatible rule.

### 69.4 "Trading In Mind: 10 Ways to Stay Focused" (Day-Trading-Mind.com, 2002, 17pp)

Ten short psychology chapters for day traders: understand the probabilistic "edge" concept, plan your
trades then trade your plan, risk small amounts, think in points not money, visualize trades mentally
before the session, be your own boss/mentor, mind your emotional language ("expense" not "loss"), trade
less (best 2 hours after open + last half-hour before close; max 3 trades/day), get a life outside
trading, keep a trading diary. Every page read (full document).

**Verdict: discarded — no agent value.** Pure psychology, day-trading-scale framing throughout (the
"first two hours after the open" and "3 strikes and you're out per day" only mean something on an
exchange with a session open/close — N/A to 24/7 continuous crypto). The trading-diary recommendation
is already exceeded by the existing orders/journal audit trail. No mechanical content.

### 69.5 "Trading for Beginners" — Trading Session Workbook (Jason Graystone, Graystone Education, 22pp)

Not a book — a **fill-in-the-blank companion workbook** for a live video "strategy session," most of
whose pages are blank templates: goals worksheets ("who do I want to be / what do I want to do / what
do I want to have"), a "market conditions" page with four unlabeled chart shapes and empty dashed boxes
next to each (to be labeled live on the call), two "real market test" pages with unlabeled charts,
market-phase (bull/bear) zig-zag diagrams with blank numbered answer lines, two candlestick-anatomy
diagrams with blank labels pointing at the wick/body, a candlestick-patterns page with four shapes and
blank labels, and roughly ten pages of blank ruled "Ideas & Notes" lines. Every page read (full
document) — there is no baked-in instructional text answering any of the blanks.

**Verdict: discarded — no agent value; excluded wholesale on the halal screen regardless.** The
document carries no self-contained content to extract (the actual teaching happens in a video this PDF
doesn't include). What little is legible even as an outline — HH/HL trend structure, candlestick
anatomy/patterns — duplicates §1/§4/§24/§25/§59 exactly. Per the standing beginner-forex caveat, retail
forex education of this kind is built on leverage/lots/margin by convention; nothing here overrides
that, so it is flagged out wholesale rather than mined line-by-line for the fragments that happen to
be visible.

### 69.6 "Competitive Benchmarking: Lessons Learned from the Trading Agent Competition" (Wolfgang Ketter & Andreas L. Symeonidis, *AI Magazine*, Summer 2012, 5pp)

**Not a trading document.** This is an academic report on the **Trading Agent Competition (TAC)**, an
AI-research benchmark series (TAC Travel, TAC SCM supply-chain management, CAT market-design, Ad
Auctions, Power TAC electricity brokering) run by the Association for Trading Agent Research to
advance multi-agent-systems research. It describes competition design, participation statistics, and
research lessons about *building agent benchmarks* — not price-trading strategy, risk management, or
market mechanics of any kind relevant to `keel`. Confirmed by full read (5pp).

**Verdict: excluded — out of scope, not about trading strategy.** No mechanical content of any kind
applies; logged only to close it out per the task brief.
