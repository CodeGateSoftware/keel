### 85.1 ⭐⭐ The three-element test for money — the analytic frame this KB was missing

The paper's spine is a clean, jurist-grade decomposition of what any candidate currency must satisfy
(3.0–3.3, restated at the Conclusion):

1. **Māl (wealth/property)** — the thing must be property at all. Two Hanafi criteria: **desirability**
   (*mayl* — human inclination toward it, i.e. it is beneficial) and **storability** (retrievable for
   later use). Crucially, **Taqi Uthmani dispels the "must be physical" objection**: the Quran/Sunnah
   never defined *māl*; later Hanafi *fatāwā* already treat **electricity and gas as *māl* despite being
   intangible**, so *"intangibles can also be Māl on condition they are desirable and retrievable."*
   ⇒ **digital-only existence is not, by itself, a disqualifier.**
2. **Taqawwum (legal value)** — the *māl* must be **lawful to use in Shariah** (*mutaqawwim*). Wine and
   pork are *māl* linguistically but not *mutaqawwim*. The test is the **default of *ibāḥah*
   (lawfulness):** *"the Aṣl and foundational premise is ʿIbāḥah in respect to items and transactions...
   Bitcoins are merely digits, there is no evidence or matter which indicates them being unlawful.
   Hence, Bitcoin has Taqawwum."*
3. **Thamaniyyah (monetary-ness)** — the property must actually **function as money** (an independent
   standard of value + unit of account, established by **Taʿāmul** (dominant usage) and **Iṣṭilāḥ**
   (public agreement)). Per Imam Muhammad's preponderant view (Taqi Uthmani), Iṣṭilāḥ is activated by
   **the public, not two transacting parties** — so widespread public adoption *as money* is required.

**Module:** `CompliancePolicy` — this is the **first clean, credentialed statement of the māl → taqawwum
→ thamaniyyah ladder** the KB has been reasoning around since §65/§67/§72. Adopt as the canonical frame
for classifying any allowlist candidate.

### 85.2 ⭐⭐ THE VERDICT — Bitcoin is *Māl* with *Taqawwum* but lacks *Thamaniyyah* → spot investing is LAWFUL

The paper's own conclusion, verbatim (p.49):

> *"Upon analysing Bitcoin to see if these criteria were met, arguably, **Bitcoin is Māl and has
> Taqawwum**. However, Bitcoin fails to fulfil the role of money... and thus **cannot be considered to
> possess Thamaniyyah**... **Nevertheless, any return on Bitcoin investments would be lawful and Shariah
> compliant according to this argument.**"*

And the abstract (p.6): *"Bitcoin does seem to be Māl (wealth) with Taqawwum (legal value), however, it
does not possess Thamaniyyah (currency attributes)... returns on Bitcoin investment would be lawful and
Shariah compliant according to this understanding."* And §7.3: *"According to this opinion, Bitcoin would
qualify as a digital asset (Māl) which possesses value (Taqawwum). From a purely fiqh and Islamic law
perspective, investing in Bitcoin and the return could be considered lawful."*

**This is EXACTLY the working position §72 said the KB already runs on — now supplied by a qualified
Mufti rather than inferred:** BTC is a **tradable digital asset (māl mutaqawwim)**, so **spot buying,
holding and selling for profit is lawful**; it is **not currency (no thamaniyyah)**, so the **strict
currency-exchange (*ṣarf* / *bay' al-ṣarf*) riba regime does NOT bind BTC↔goods or BTC↔fiat spot
trades** (that regime attaches to *thaman* — gold, silver, fiat — not to a bare tradable asset).

⭐ **The māl-vs-derivative distinction the paper draws (§7.1) is the load-bearing insight and directly
supports admission:** *"The Bitcoins in themselves are the assets, whereas derivatives... are nothing
in themselves but representations of the price fluctuations of underlying assets. The value of Bitcoin
is in itself and not in an underlying asset."* This is precisely why BTC/ETH clear our screen while the
excluded instruments (CFDs, futures, options — §4/§10/§18/§65.11) do not: **BTC is the ʿayn-like thing
being traded; a derivative is a bet on a price.** Grounds `HARAM_SECTORS`/derivative exclusions in a
positive reason, not just a prohibition list.

= `native` (a base-layer coin, neither warehouse-receipt `ayn` nor issuer-claim `dayn`), `pays_yield =

§71's IIFA non-ruling stands.

### 85.3 The three scholarly opinions, and why the paper lands on "digital asset"

§7.1 lays out the field cleanly (attributed to the author's personal discussions with scholars):

- **Opinion 1 — Bitcoin is NOT *māl*** (pure speculation, non-compliant): argues Bitcoin is *"just
  numbers... no intrinsic function, utility at all,"* likening it to settling price differences = a
  form of **maisir**. The paper takes this seriously (*"has some weight"*) but rejects it on the
  māl-vs-derivative distinction above — BTC has value *in itself*, unlike a derivative.
- **Opinion 2 — Bitcoin is a digital asset, not money.** ← **the paper's adopted position.**
- **Opinion 3 — Bitcoin is currency** (has *thamaniyyah*): rejected, because BTC is **not an independent
  standard of value** (it is priced *in* fiat), and its **volatility defeats money's stabilising
  purpose** — *"Something unstable cannot bring stability to others"* (§7.1–§7.2, and the five reasons
  in §7.2: lacks thamaniyyah; the *ṣifāt ʿāriḍa* → *al-ʿadm* legal maxim [changeable attributes default
  to non-existence, so "currency" can't be attributed without positive evidence]; the risk load; fails
  Maqāṣid; no overriding *ʿUrf* treating it as money since people invest in it rather than spend it).

**Note for us:** the project has never claimed BTC/ETH are *currency* — the engine trades them as
**spot assets against USD/USDC**, which is Opinion 2's exact treatment. The refutation of Opinion 3 is
therefore not a problem for us; it just confirms we must **not** apply *ṣarf* rules to BTC/ETH (we don't).

### 85.4 ⭐ The second layer — Maqāṣid / Islamic moral economy: lawful, but "not ideal." Record honestly.

The paper does **not** stop at "fiqh-lawful." It runs Bitcoin through the **Maqāṣid al-Shariah**
preservation-of-wealth screen (§5–§6) and the **Islamic moral economy** framework (§7.3), where Bitcoin
**fails several dimensions**: *marketability/circulation* (volatility → hoarding not spending),
*transparency* (anonymous, cryptic network), **ḥifẓ al-Māl** (weak regulatory safeguards → hacker/fraud
exposure; a large-economy ban could crash it), *equity (ʿadl)*, and **real-economy linkage** (crypto
*"does not serve the real economy... does not produce real goods, services or jobs"*).

Hence the paper's **two-tier verdict** (abstract + §7.3): fiqh-**permissible**, but Bitcoin is *"not
ideal as a long-term investment,"* the Islamic-finance industry *"should not consider its use in
exchange unless there is a specific need until a regulated and transparent framework is established,"*
and *"concentrating all of one's wealth into digital assets can have more harm... than good."*

⚠️ **DO NOT cherry-pick the permissive half.** The honest reading is: **halal to trade, but a
prudence/*makrūh-*adjacent caution against over-concentration and treating it as a wealth-preservation
vehicle.** This maps onto — and independently vindicates — several standing project postures:
- **Small fixed-fractional sizing** (`risk_pct=0.01`, §54.18/§83.5/§84) and the **total-exposure /
  per-asset caps** (rails 4/6/7) directly answer *"do not concentrate all wealth"* — keel is a
  **small-allocation** spot agent, never an all-in wealth store.
- **PAXG as the diversifier / the real-asset leg** (§83.10, `UNCORRELATED_ASSETS={"PAXG"}`) answers the
  real-economy/ḥifẓ-al-Māl critique better than a BTC-only book would — gold *is* asset-backed and a
  classical store of value.
- The **2017 "until a regulated/transparent framework is established" caveat has substantially aged**:
  spot BTC/ETH now trade on regulated venues (Coinbase Advanced, our broker) under maturing regimes —
  exactly the condition the paper set. **The 2026 Amanah update (flagged §85.0) should be fetched to
  confirm the author's current read** before leaning hard on the permissive verdict for a live decision.

### 85.5 The thaman-vs-māl split sharpens BTC/ETH vs PAXG — and the staking exclusion

The paper's framework cleanly separates our allowlist by *type of thing*:

- **BTC / ETH = *māl mutaqawwim*, NOT *thaman*** → traded as bare spot assets; **no *bay' al-ṣarf*
  regime** (no same-session hand-to-hand constraint beyond ordinary spot *qabd*, §67.1). This is the
  paper's central holding.
- **PAXG = a claim on allocated physical GOLD**, and **gold is *al-thaman al-khilqī*** (§2.4 — intrinsic,
  God-given *thamaniyyah* that *"can never be deactivated"*). So PAXG **does** trigger the stricter
  **§65.5 *bay' al-ṣarf*** regime (immediate settlement, no deferment) — *because of the gold backing,
  not despite it.* §85 thus **grounds the BTC/ETH-vs-PAXG asymmetry the screen already encodes**
  (`backing: native` vs `backing: ayn` + the ṣarf warning) in the paper's own money taxonomy.
- **Staking / PoS yield:** the paper (and §72.3, §65.14) treats a return-for-holding as riba-like. Bare
  spot BTC/ETH earns nothing (`pays_yield=false` is correct); **staked ETH / stETH would be
  `riba_yield` and excluded** — our attestation must state it covers **unstaked spot only.**

**Module:** `compliance/screen.py` — no code change; §85 is the **fiqh justification** for the existing
`BACKING_NATIVE` (BTC/ETH) vs `BACKING_AYN`+ṣarf-warning (PAXG) split and the `pays_yield` gate.
