### 86.1 ⭐⭐ The reframe — from the 2017 money-test to a rights-based *Ḥaqq → Māl Ḥukmī* screen

§85 (2017) asked *"is Bitcoin money?"* (Māl / Taqawwum / Thamaniyyah). §86 (2026) asks the better
question — *"what kind of legal thing is a digital asset, and is trading it lawful?"* — and answers it
with a cleaner construct:

- **A digital asset is a *Ḥaqq* (right).** Owning it = exercising **exclusive, protocol-recognised
  control** over a ledger position: *"only the holder of the private key can sign the transactions
  that change it."* That exclusive control (*Ikhtiṣāṣ*) is a right, and the right is a **gateway to
  *Manfaʿah*** (transfer value, make payments, access dApps, participate in finance) — Figure 2.
- **That *Ḥaqq* becomes *Māl Ḥukmī* (constructive/legal property)** when it is: **valuable, munḍabiṭ
  (well-defined), maʿlūm (known), free of jahālah (ambiguity), lawfully useful, deliverable via *qabḍ*
  as effective control, and recognised/dealt-in by a body of people (*ʿUrf*).** Then *"its ownership
  and trade are lawful."*
- **The bit-level argument (Figure 3):** everything digital reduces to bits; *"Fiqh does not judge the
  arrangement of bits — it looks to the utility a thing delivers."* Denying *māliyyah* to digital
  things would void the **entire digital economy** (software licences, domains, e-books, cloud, phone
  numbers) — *"No one holds such an opinion."* Intangibility is **not** a disqualifier.

**Module:** `CompliancePolicy` — this is a **stronger, more operational frame than §85** for classifying
allowlist candidates. Adopt it as primary; §85's money-ladder becomes the historical/secondary lens.

### 86.2 ⭐⭐ THE 2026 VERDICT — "Crypto is not impermissible by nature; qualifying digital assets are lawful to own and trade"

The Conclusion, verbatim (pp.37–38):

> *"Accordingly, once a digital right is valuable, identifiable, exclusive, capable of effective control
> (Iḥrāz), preservable for future use (Iddikhār), deliverable through Qabḍ in the form of effective
> control, transferable, recognised by ʿUrf and dealt with in contracts, it takes the ruling of **Māl
> Ḥukmī — constructive and legal property — and its ownership and trade are lawful.**"*
>
> *"**Crypto is not impermissible by nature.** Digital assets are Ḥuqūq which, when the conditions above
> are met, become **Māl Ḥukmī: lawful to own, lawful to trade** and capable of full recognition within
> the categories of the Ḥanafī school."*

⭐ **This RESOLVES §85's standing caveat.** §85 (2017) hedged: *"not ideal as a long-term investment...
the industry should not consider its use unless there is a specific need until a regulated and
transparent framework is established."* §86 (2026) explicitly treats that condition as **met** — the
Conclusion cites *"legal recognition across major jurisdictions, adoption at institutional scale and a
growing body of genuine use cases"* as the *ʿUrf* that now establishes *Tamawwul*. **The moral-economy
hesitation is superseded by nine years of market maturation.** ⇒ **for the BTC/ETH attestation, §86 is
the primary, current, permissive authority; §85 is its earlier, more-hedged predecessor.**

⭐ **The permissibility threshold is LOW (Abū Ḥanīfah's method):** *"a single genuine and lawful use
suffices, even where it is not the dominant use. If even a musical instrument could be deemed Māl on
the strength of a secondary use, then digital assets — which enable the unbanked to save, remittances
to reach poorer countries and the financially excluded to invest — qualify as Māl with far greater
ease."* BTC (store of value / borderless settlement) and ETH (compute / smart contracts) clear this
by a wide margin.

### 86.3 ⭐ The three-layer screen — a ready-made structure for `keel assets` (infrastructure / application / asset)

The paper's operational core (Ch 2, Figure 1): *"Before any specific digital asset can be deemed Halal
it must undergo Shariah screening... across three layers — a failure at any single layer fails the
whole asset."*

1. **Infrastructure (where it runs):** base protocol + consensus + any L2s/sidechains relied on. *"If
   the infrastructure exists solely to enable unlawful activity... it is not compliant. Where the base
   layer is neutral and merely hosts a range of uses, the infrastructure alone does not disqualify the
   native asset."* → BTC/ETH base layers are neutral general-purpose networks → **pass.**
2. **Application (what it does):** the service/use-case. Only-unlawful-service → fail.
3. **Asset (what you own):** the token's utility, economics and **legal nature in Fiqh**. *"If the
   token... represents a right that is itself impermissible, it is not compliant."*

⭐ **This maps almost 1:1 onto keel's EXISTING screen and sharpens it.** `compliance/screen.py` today
checks sector (`HARAM_SECTORS`), backing (`native`/`ayn`/`dayn`), and yield — that is essentially the
**Asset layer**. §86 adds two named layers the screen does *not* yet encode — **Infrastructure** and
**Application** — plus the *ʿUrf*/adoption test. **Candidate refinement (not yet built, logged):** extend
the attestation record with infrastructure + application classifications so a chain built solely for
haram use (e.g. a gambling-only L1) fails even if the token itself looks clean. For our current
allowlist (BTC/ETH/PAXG on neutral base layers) all three layers pass; the value is for future
candidates. See spec §3.3.

### 86.4 ⭐⭐ "Not every token qualifies as Māl" — the four categories, and the DIRECT rulings on DOGE- and USDT-class tokens

Ch 2 divides digital things into **four categories** and is explicit that Shariah *"does not need to
approve or reject every digital token as a single class"* — **asset by asset**:

1. Directly used/enjoyed (spreadsheet, game, e-book) — IP-like.
2. Instruments giving access (domain, API key, airline ticket).
3. Registered/allocated positions (domain via registry, licence via regulator).
4. **Digital CLAIMS — "gift cards, loyalty points, money held by a custodian, and tokens the issuer
   promises to redeem... in legal substance, claims against the issuer"** → **governed by the rules of
   *Dayn* (debt).**

Two of the paper's own disqualifiers land **directly on assets in our decision queue:**

- ⛔ **"A token that has no genuine use or benefit and survives only because people hope to sell it to
  someone else at a higher price may FAIL to qualify as *Māl*."** (p.19; Conclusion: *"a token with no
  genuine utility... is not Shariah compliant"*; asset-level screen p.36: must have *"a genuine
  function, honest demand, recognisable utility and a sufficiently stable form of economic
  acceptance"*). **⇒ This is the sharpest KB statement yet for the DOGE decision** — a memecoin whose
  thesis is pure resale-hope, no genuine utility, is exactly the failure case the paper names. **Strong
  lean: EXCLUDE DOGE.** (§28.4's "screen the underlying purpose" now has a named, Mufti-grade backing.)
- ⚠️ **Fiat-pegged stablecoins are category-4 *Dayn* (a claim on the issuer's reserves).** The paper's
  own institutional context is a **Darul Uloom Karachi fatwa PROHIBITING USDT**. **⇒ This bears
  directly on keel's USDC quote/settlement currency** (`config.quote_currency`, rail 13 USDC-funding):
  USDC is structurally a claim on Circle's reserves = *dayn*. **Not necessarily impermissible** —
  holding a sound, non-interest-bearing claim is generally fine, and keel earns **no yield** on USDC
  (yield would be riba) — but it is a **live open compliance question flagged, not settled**: whether
  our settlement currency should be a fiat-pegged stablecoin at all, given the highest Deobandi
  institution has just prohibited the sister asset. **Recorded for the user; NOT a blocker today.** See
  §86.6.

### 86.5 ⭐ Native BTC/ETH are *Māl Ḥukmī*, NOT *Dayn* — and PAXG's classification gets HARDER, not easier

Appendix 6 (§Appx6.14 "Why native digital assets are not necessarily Dayn"; §Appx6.15 "Why the right
has become Māl Ḥukmī") is the load-bearing distinction for our allowlist:

- **BTC / ETH = native ledger positions, no issuer standing behind them** → a *Ḥaqq* of exclusive
  control that becomes ***Māl Ḥukmī***, **NOT a claim on anyone (not *dayn*).** This is the affirmative
  ground for admitting them: sector `native`, backing `native`, genuine utility, *ʿUrf* adoption. **The
  strongest, most current authority for the BTC/ETH attestation.**
- ⚠️ **PAXG gets classified more carefully under §86, and possibly HARDER.** §85 admitted PAXG as *ayn*
  (allocated gold, §65.5 *bay' al-ṣarf*). But §86's **category 4** says *"money/asset held by a
  custodian and tokens the issuer promises to redeem"* are **claims against the issuer = *dayn*.**
  PAXG is a Paxos-issued token redeemable for vaulted gold — **so the ʿayn-vs-dayn call turns on the
  actual legal structure**: is each PAXG *allocated ownership of specific gold* (→ *ayn/māl*, ṣarf
  applies) or *a redemption claim on Paxos* (→ *dayn*, and then §86's debt-rules + our screen's
  `dayn`-rejection would BLOCK it)? **§86 raises the bar on PAXG — verify the Paxos allocation/title
  structure before attesting, do not assume `ayn`.** (This tightens, and partly reopens, §72.4's
  OneGram-corroborated `ayn` reading.)

### 86.6 The three guiding principles + reconciliation with §71/§72/§85

**Ch 1's three principles** (the methodological backbone, all pro-permissibility):
1. **Taḥarrī lil-Jawāz** — the Ḥanafī jurists actively *seek a tenable permissive reading* of things
   that look unlawful on the surface (al-Marghīnānī, Qāḍī Khān: *"a rational person intends validity,
   not voidness"*).
2. **The Aṣl in muʿāmalāt is Ibāḥah** — transactions are permissible by default; *"it is always upon
   those of the view that something is impermissible to prove it decisively."* (Burden on the prohibiter.)
3. **Fiqh rulings are reached through Fiqh's own constructs** — a non-specialist cannot reliably reach
   correct rulings; *ḥawālah* is *"movement"* of a debt though nothing physical moves. (I.e. distrust
   surface "it's just numbers / *farzi*" objections.)

**Reconciliation:**
- **vs §85 (same author, 2017):** §86 **supersedes** it on the caveat (permissive verdict no longer
  hedged on "wait for regulation") and **replaces** the money-test frame with the rights-based frame.
  Keep §85 as the earlier, more-cautious datapoint and the origin of the moral-economy prudence (which
  keel still honours via sizing/exposure caps — that prudence is *good practice*, not a prohibition
  §86 lifts).
- **vs §71 (IIFA non-ruling) / §72 (split academic literature):** §86 is a far stronger *individual*
  authority (presented at Darul Uloom Karachi under Taqi Uthmani's direction) — but **still an
  individual ijtihad inside an OPEN institutional process**, and that process has already **prohibited
  USDT**. So the KB's honest posture is UNCHANGED in kind: **a strong, current, named permissive
  authority for native assets (BTC/ETH), under an institution that has NOT issued a final crypto ruling
  and leans restrictive on stablecoins.** Do not report the question as "settled."
